# Step 11: Notifications (at least 2 channels or providers)

**Inputs:** Statement §3.2 (regulation requires notifying movements; **at least 2** notification systems, external or own), Step 0 (FR-09, QAS-04: secondary provider delivers within 60 s, none lost), Step 2 (notify **every monetary transaction by mobile messaging and email simultaneously**; notify every login, lockouts, risk rejections, contact changes to old and new contacts; mask account numbers; alternate providers for critical services), Step 4 (Notification service, T2), Step 8 (Kafka, outbox), Step 10 (phone/email notified is part of the audit record).

---

## 1. Channels and what each is for

| Channel                     | Role                                                                                                                  | Regulatory?                                                                           |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **SMS**               | "Mobile messaging" for every monetary transaction, login, lockout, contact change, OTP                                | **Yes**                                                                         |
| **Email**             | Sent **at the same time** as the SMS for every monetary transaction; also statements and contact-change notices | **Yes**                                                                         |
| **Push** (FCM / APNs) | Instant, free, rich; transfer status updates (FR-13)                                                                  | No: complement. Whether push can replace SMS is a question for the SB (see section 8) |
| **In-app inbox**      | History of all notifications inside the app                                                                           | No                                                                                    |

So the **2 notification systems** the statement asks for are **SMS + email**, sent in parallel. In addition, the **SMS channel has 2 providers** with automatic failover, because SMS carries OTPs and the SB asks for alternate providers for critical services.

## 2. Provider options

| Provider                                                                                | Channel             | Notes                                                                                         | Status                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Local SMS aggregator** (direct connections to Claro, Movistar, CNT)             | SMS                 | Usually the best price and delivery in Ecuador; SMPP / HTTP API. Bank-grade SLA to negotiate. | Active                                                                                                                                                            |
| **AWS End User Messaging SMS**                                                    | SMS, push, OTP      | AWS-native, pay per message, delivery events to EventBridge / Kinesis.                        | Active. **Amazon Pinpoint reached end of support on 30 Oct 2026**; its SMS, push and OTP APIs continue here. Don't build on Pinpoint campaigns or journeys. |
| Twilio / Infobip / Vonage                                                               | SMS, push, WhatsApp | Global aggregators, good APIs, higher price per SMS to Ecuador                                | Active                                                                                                                                                            |
| **Amazon SES**                                                                    | Email               | Low cost, dedicated IPs, DKIM, delivery events                                                | Active                                                                                                                                                            |
| SendGrid (Twilio)                                                                       | Email               | Mature, good deliverability tooling                                                           | Active                                                                                                                                                            |
| **FCM / APNs** (direct, or through AWS End User Messaging Push / SNS mobile push) | Push                | Free; FCM requires the HTTP v1 API (the legacy API was shut down in 2024)                     | Active                                                                                                                                                            |
| Azure Communication Services / Notification Hubs                                        | SMS, email, push    | Other cloud (ADR-002)                                                                         | Active                                                                                                                                                            |

**Ecuador SMS specifics:** carrier handling of alphanumeric sender IDs differs (reports say Claro and Movistar overwrite them with a short code, CNT keeps them). Transactional and OTP traffic should be registered with each carrier through the aggregator. ARCOTEL is the telecom regulator. A local aggregator handles this better than a global one.

## 3. ADR-016: Notification channels and providers

**Status:** Proposed

**Decision.**

| Channel | Primary                                                                                        | Secondary (failover)                                                                           |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SMS     | **Local aggregator** with direct carrier connections (Provider A in the context diagram) | **AWS End User Messaging SMS** (or a global aggregator such as Twilio / Infobip)         |
| Email   | **Amazon SES** (us-east-1) (Provider B in the context diagram)                           | Amazon SES in us-east-2 (same provider, other region); SendGrid if a second vendor is required |
| Push    | FCM (Android) / APNs (iOS), direct                                                             | — (non-regulatory; SMS + email already sent)                                                  |

### Justifications

1. **Two independent systems for every regulatory notice.** SMS and email travel over different networks and providers, so the failure of one doesn't leave the customer uninformed. This is the "at least 2 notification systems" of the statement and the "mobile messaging and email simultaneously" of the SB rule.
2. **Failover where it matters most.** SMS carries OTPs (login and step-up, Step 6): if SMS fails, customers can't log in or transfer. Two SMS providers with automatic failover meet QAS-04 (delivered within 60 s) and the SB's alternate-provider rule.
3. **Cost and delivery fit Ecuador.** Local aggregators usually offer much lower prices per SMS and better carrier delivery than global platforms. At ~225 M notices a month (~164 M monetary transactions plus ~60 M login notices, Step 0), the price per SMS is one of the largest running costs of the platform (section 7).
4. **No dependence on retired services.** Amazon Pinpoint's engagement features ended on 30 Oct 2026; we only use the messaging APIs that continue under AWS End User Messaging.

## 4. Component design

```text
 Kafka (events): transfers.*, interbank.*, security.*, customer.*, device.*, onboarding.*
          │
          ▼
 ┌──────────────────────────── Notification Service (EKS) ────────────────────────────┐
 │ Event Consumer (idempotent: eventId)                                                │
 │   └► Notification Policy (which events → which channels; regulatory = SMS + email)  │
 │   └► Recipient Resolver (contact data from Customer Profile, cached; old+new contact│
 │        on contact change)                                                           │
 │   └► Template Renderer (versioned Spanish templates, account masking, no links in   │
 │        SMS)                                                                         │
 │   └► Dispatcher ──► priority lanes (Kafka topics):                                  │
 │          notify.sms.p0 (OTP, security)   notify.sms.p1 (transactions)               │
 │          notify.email.p1                 notify.push.p2                             │
 │   └► Channel Senders, one per channel, each with Provider Adapters:                 │
 │          SMS:   Local aggregator adapter ──(circuit breaker, health score)──┐       │
 │                 AWS End User Messaging adapter ◄─────── failover ───────────┘       │
 │          Email: SES us-east-1 adapter ◄─► SES us-east-2 adapter                     │
 │          Push:  FCM adapter, APNs adapter                                           │
 │   └► Delivery Tracker (DynamoDB): sent / delivered / failed, provider message ID    │
 │        ◄── delivery receipts (SMS DLR webhooks, SES events, FCM/APNs responses)     │
 │   └► Retry topics + DLQ (alert)                                                     │
 └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
          │ NotificationSent / NotificationFailed events
          ▼
     Audit (Step 10), Observability (Step 14)
```

| Pattern                                         | Where                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Publish / Subscribe**                   | Notification is one subscriber of domain events; senders never call it directly                                   |
| **Strategy + Adapter**                    | One adapter per provider behind a common `send()` interface; the strategy picks the provider by health and cost |
| **Circuit breaker + failover**            | Per provider adapter                                                                                              |
| **Priority queues**                       | OTP and security messages never wait behind a burst of transaction notices                                        |
| **Idempotent consumer / idempotency key** | `notificationId = eventId + channel`, passed to providers as client reference                                   |
| **Template Method**                       | Same rendering pipeline for every channel, channel-specific formatting                                            |
| **Retry with backoff + DLQ**              | Transient provider errors                                                                                         |

## 5. Failover and delivery rules

| Situation                                                                                 | Behaviour                                                                                                                                            |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Primary SMS provider returns an error or times out (2 s)                                  | Retry once, then send via the secondary. Circuit opens after repeated failures; all SMS go to the secondary until the primary's health check passes. |
| SMS accepted but **no delivery receipt within 45 s** for an OTP or security message | Resend through the secondary (meets the 60 s target of QAS-04). A rare duplicate is accepted; a lost OTP is not.                                     |
| Transaction SMS without delivery receipt                                                  | Recorded as "unconfirmed"; the email and push for the same event are already sent. Resend via secondary only on an explicit failure.                 |
| Email bounce                                                                              | Mark the email as unverified in Customer Profile; ask the customer to update it at next login.                                                       |
| Both SMS providers down                                                                   | Email + push still go out; incident raised (critical).                                                                                               |
| Region failover                                                                           | The Notification service runs in us-east-2 (warm standby, Step 13); SES us-east-2 is already configured.                                             |

**Ordering:** the SMS and email for one event are dispatched **in parallel**, not one after the other, to meet the "simultaneously" rule.

**Throughput:** payday peak ~500 monetary TPS (QAS-09) → ~1,000 regulatory messages per second (SMS + email). Kafka absorbs bursts; provider contracts must cover the peak TPS, and senders respect each provider's rate limit.

## 6. Content and security rules

| Rule                                         | Implementation                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mask account numbers (Step 2)                | `****1234` in all templates                                                                       |
| Minimum content of a transaction notice      | Type, amount, masked origin/destination, date and time, transaction number, channel                 |
| **No links in SMS**                    | Reduces phishing risk; customers are told the bank never sends links by SMS                         |
| Email authentication                         | **SPF, DKIM, DMARC (`p=reject`)** on the bank's sending domain; dedicated sending subdomain |
| Contact change                               | Notify **old and new** phone/email (Step 2)                                                   |
| Login notice                                 | Every successful e-banking login (Step 2), plus lockouts                                            |
| Customer can't opt out of regulatory notices | Preferences only control the extra channels (push, in-app)                                          |
| Personal data with providers                 | Processing agreements (LOPDP); providers receive only phone/email and the rendered text             |
| Audit                                        | Phone and email notified, provider, status and timestamps go to the audit record (Step 10)          |

## 7. Cost (first estimate; detailed in Step 15)

| Item               | Estimate                                                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Regulatory notices | ~164 M monetary transactions + ~60 M logins a month (Step 0) → ~225 M SMS + ~225 M emails                  |
| Email (SES)        | ~USD 0.10 per 1,000 → **~USD 22.5 k a month**                                                                   |
| SMS                | Depends almost entirely on the negotiated price: at USD 0.005 → ~USD 1.1 M a month; at USD 0.02 → ~USD 4.5 M a month |
| Push               | Free (FCM / APNs)                                                                                                      |

SMS is the dominant cost. Levers:

1. Negotiate bank-volume prices with a local aggregator (the main reason for ADR-016's primary choice).
2. Ask the SB whether **push** to an authorized, bound device (Step 6) counts as "mobile messaging" for customers who opt in. If it does, SMS can be kept for OTP, security notices and customers without the app.
3. Group very small, frequent movements only if the regulation allows it.

## 8. Open questions

- Does the SB accept **push notifications** as the "mobile messaging" channel? (Large cost impact, section 7.)
- Can transaction notifications be charged to customers or configured by amount threshold? Some banks in the region do this; check the current SB rule.
- Carrier registration of the sender ID / short code for transactional and OTP traffic (aggregator task).
